# Peerbots Ecosystem Governance

The Peerbots open-source ecosystem is governed by **[Peerbots, Inc.](https://www.peerbots.org/)**, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization. Peerbots partners with educators, therapists, researchers, advisors, and community adopters to guide our roadmap.

## Guiding Principles

All Peerbots repositories and product decisions follow three core principles:

1. **Inclusive, customizable, human-centered design**: Social robots should adapt to diverse learners, clinicians, and families — not the other way around.
2. **Data privacy and human control by default**: Collect no unnecessary user data, and keep humans firmly in control of robot interactions.
3. **Research-informed**: Platform design, hardware, and roadmaps are informed by academic research and real-world practice.

## Reviewing Major Proposals

When evaluating a major feature proposal or new project idea, maintainers ask four questions:

- Does this idea increase inclusion or customizability across the Peerbots ecosystem?
- Does this idea ensure no unnecessary user data is collected?
- Does this idea ensure that people retain meaningful control?
- Is this idea informed by existing or ongoing research, or real-world practitioner needs?

## Decision Making

- **Day-to-day maintenance** (bug fixes, docs, accessibility, dependency updates): Reviewed and merged by repository maintainers via Pull Request.
- **Cross-repo features & architectural changes**: Discussed openly in [Peerbots GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/PEERbots/.github/discussions) or via a **Project Proposal** issue, tracked on the [Peerbots Org Roadmap Project](https://github.com/orgs/PEERbots/projects).
- **Licensing, security & nonprofit stewardship**: Maintained by Peerbots, Inc. leadership in accordance with our 501(c)(3) charter and [FLA 2.0 open-source guarantee](./CLA.md).
